# Protocol Generator Skill

## Purpose
Generates ready-to-use colostrum feeding protocols, sanitation checklists, and cold-weather action plans based on farm conditions. Outputs printable, step-by-step instructions for farm staff.

---

## Standard Feeding Protocols

- Ensure that all calves are fed colostrum within the first hour of birth.
- Use a clean bucket or bottle — never use feeding equipment that has not been sanitized.
- Follow recommended amounts based on calf birth weight (target 8–10% of body weight for first feeding).
- Always confirm colostrum quality with a Brix refractometer before feeding.
- Record feeding time, volume, calf ID, and Brix reading for every calf.

---

## Colostrum Protocols

### Quality Assessment
- Assess colostrum quality with a Brix refractometer before feeding.
  - ≥ 22% Brix = Excellent (feed first)
  - 18–21% Brix = Good (acceptable, feed promptly)
  - 15–17% Brix = Fair (supplement or blend with higher-quality colostrum)
  - < 15% Brix = Poor (do not feed as sole source — use colostrum replacer)
- Ensure colostrum is from the same species as the calf.
- Heat colostrum to 40–42°C (104–108°F) before feeding — never microwave.
- Do not feed colostrum that has been heat-treated above 60°C (140°F) as IgG degrades.

### Timing Windows
- **0–2 hours:** Maximum IgG absorption — highest priority feeding window.
- **2–6 hours:** Good absorption — feed immediately if 0–2 hr window was missed.
- **6–12 hours:** Declining absorption — feed as soon as possible, consider tube feeding if calf is weak.
- **> 12 hours:** Gut closure beginning — feeding still provides local gut immunity and nutrition, but systemic IgG uptake is severely reduced.

### Volume Targets
- First feeding: 8–10% of birth weight
  - 80 lb calf → 3.2–4.0 qt
  - 90 lb calf → 3.6–4.5 qt
  - 100 lb calf → 4.0–5.0 qt
- Second feeding: repeat 4–6 hours after first feeding if calf is vigorous and standing.

## Troubleshooting Action Plans

- **Calf refuses to drink:** Check for signs of weakness, scours, or respiratory distress. Tube-feed if calf is unable to nurse on its own within 30 minutes.
- **Low Brix reading (< 15%):** Switch to a commercial colostrum replacer providing ≥ 100g IgG per dose.
- **Colostrum unavailable:** Use a colostrum replacer — not a supplement — that meets USDA standards (≥ 100g IgG per dose).
- **Failure to thrive after first 24 hours:** Evaluate IgG transfer success (serum total protein ≥ 5.5 g/dL at 24–48 hours of age). Consult veterinarian if below threshold.
- **Calf born weak or hypothermic:** Warm calf to ≥ 100°F rectal temperature before tube feeding. Colostrum absorption is impaired in hypothermic calves.

## Sanitation Checklists

### Before Feeding
- [ ] Wash hands with soap and warm water
- [ ] Inspect all feeding equipment for cracks, residue, or odor
- [ ] Clean buckets and bottles with hot water and approved dairy detergent
- [ ] Rinse thoroughly — detergent residue kills beneficial bacteria and irritates calf gut

### After Feeding
- [ ] Rinse equipment immediately with warm water to prevent protein bonding
- [ ] Wash with hot soapy water, scrubbing all surfaces
- [ ] Sanitize with an approved acid sanitizer or iodine-based solution
- [ ] Air dry — never towel dry (reintroduces bacteria)
- [ ] Store inverted in a clean, dry location

## Cold Weather Protocols

### Adjustments for Temperatures Below 50°F (10°C)
- Increase feeding volume or energy density — calves burn more energy staying warm.
- Feed colostrum warmed to 104–108°F (40–42°C) — cold colostrum chills the calf's core.
- Do not delay feeding to warm a cold calf — warm AND feed simultaneously when possible.

### Signs of Hypothermia to Watch For
- Rectal temperature < 100°F (37.8°C)
- Shivering (early stage) or absence of shivering (severe — calf has lost ability to thermoregulate)
- Weakness, inability to stand, or failure to nurse

### Cold Weather Action Steps
1. Move calf to a warm, draft-free area immediately.
2. Warm calf with calf warmer box, heat lamp, or warm water bath (100–102°F water).
3. Do NOT feed until rectal temp reaches ≥ 100°F — aspiration risk is high in cold, weak calves.
4. Once stable, tube-feed warmed colostrum at full volume target.
5. Monitor for 2–4 hours post-feeding for signs of recovery.
6. Ensure bedding depth is adequate (the "nesting score" — calf legs should not be visible when lying).

### Bedding Nesting Score Guide
- **Score 1 (Poor):** No bedding, legs fully visible — inadequate
- **Score 2 (Moderate):** Legs partially covered — marginal
- **Score 3 (Good):** Legs not visible when calf is lying — target standard
